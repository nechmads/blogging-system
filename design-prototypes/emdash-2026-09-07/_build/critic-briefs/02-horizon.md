CHOSEN DIRECTION — "Horizon": the publication is called Looking Ahead, so time is the spine of the site. A large date axis runs across the top of the home page with month/year numerals as the largest type on the page; posts hang from their real dates; "now" at the right end is the only thing in colour; the lead headline is pinned to now. Empty stretches of time are compressed with a marked axis break and clustered dates are staggered rather than spaced evenly. Scrolling the archive travels back along the axis with a cursor tracking the reader's position. The article page keeps a thin sticky axis strip with the post's date marked and a reading-progress line. Images, when present, are small square annotations pinned to the axis with a leader line; when absent the tick carries only its date. World translated: the forecaster's chart room (tide tables, almanacs, ruled scales) — with weather icons, roadmap dots-and-cards, chart chrome and monospaced type rejected.

INTENDED FEELING: calm foresight — a chart table, not a dashboard.

SIGNATURE IDEA: giant light numerals with a single coloured "now" marker, and the archive as travel back in time.

TYPE ROLES: Schibsted Grotesk for numerals (large, light), titles (the only bold role), and UI; Literata for the article body and italic decks.

MUST NOT COLLAPSE INTO: a vertical timeline with dots and cards; a conventional navbar; a hero image; a product-roadmap look; small-caps or monospaced metadata; even spacing that misrepresents the dates.
