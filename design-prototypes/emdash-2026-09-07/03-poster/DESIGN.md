# 03 — Poster (draft design notes for this prototype only)

Static prototype for the Hot Metal / EmDash template round two. Two pages,
`index.html` (home) and `post.html` (article), generated by `_build/c3.py`
from the shared sample content. Everything is embedded or relative
(`fonts/`, `img/`); nothing loads from the network. Not a product-wide
design system.

## Originality gate

- **Probable generic answer to refuse:** a centred hero headline with a
  small deck under it and a card grid of posts; serif display + mono
  metadata; hairline rules; a top-left wordmark; images duotoned into slots.
- **Content-native rule that generates the composition:** *claim → evidence*.
  A post is a claim, the article is its evidence. The claim gets scale (the
  lead title consumes the first screen; the archive is a wall of claims set
  at poster size, alternating left/right, separated by whitespace only); the
  evidence gets measure (a calm 66ch Geist column where poster scale returns
  only in two pull quotes). Imagery is never part of the claim: on desktop an
  image exists only while you reach for a title (hover/focus); on touch and
  narrow screens it is a 72px square beside the deck. A missing image
  therefore has no state to design.
- **First-viewport silhouette and type roles:** a black mass of condensed
  letterforms filling the width from the left edge, ragged right; a small
  grey deck block bottom-right with one accent-tinted phrase; the publication
  name running vertically up the right rail; a four-word nav cluster fixed
  bottom-right. One family (Bricolage Grotesque) for every display and UI
  role, hierarchy carried by the `opsz`/`wdth`/`wght` axes and scale; Geist
  only for body copy, comments and forms. Heavy weight in exactly one role
  per page (titles / pull quotes).
- **Fallbacks that would invalidate it:** outline or gradient text, a centred
  hero, cards or chips, a hero image, mono anywhere, hairline rules on the
  home page, small-caps labels, a second bold role, a numbered index.
- **How the premise serves the job:** the home page's one job is to make one
  article irresistible and the archive scannable — the claim at poster scale
  is the pitch, the wall lets a reader scan seven claims by title alone with
  date-first metadata beside each. The article page deliberately drops the
  volume so 1,700 words read comfortably.

## Tokens

| token | value | role |
|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | `#fff` | ground |
| `--ink` | `#000` | all text, focus ring, buttons |
| `--grey` | `#595959` | the one secondary-text grey (7.0:1 on white) |
| `--publication-accent` | injected on `<html>`; defaults to `#b4361f` | never used raw |
| `--accent-tint` | `oklch(from var(--publication-accent) 0.93 min(c, 0.08) h)` | marker highlight behind the deck's key phrase; the hover fill on article links |
| `--accent-floor` | `oklch(from var(--publication-accent) clamp(0.2, l, 0.55) c h)` | defined and measured, currently unused: links underline in ink so the accent appears exactly once per screen |
| `--rail` | `6rem` desktop, `0` under 760px | reserved right column for masthead + nav |
| `--gutter` | `2.5rem` desktop, `1rem` narrow | right page edge (before the rail), wide enough that right-aligned band text never reads as one stack with the nav cluster |
| `--edge` | `5rem` desktop, `1rem` narrow | left page edge — sized to answer the 6rem rail so the poster reads as framed, not cramped |
| `--display` | Bricolage Grotesque (variable: opsz 12–96, wdth 75–100, wght 200–800) | every display and UI role |
| `--body` | Geist (variable wght 100–900) | article body, comments, forms |

Type roles:

- `.poster` — titles and pull quotes: wght 800, `font-stretch: 84%`,
  letter-spacing −0.035em (−0.025em at wall size, where the ExtraBold starts
  to clot), line-height 0.9, `opsz 96`.
- Small tier, 0.9375rem, wdth 100, `opsz 12`, lowercase, black: metadata,
  tags (middot-separated, no underline, no colour) and form labels at wght
  400; only the nav cluster, its current-page underline and "Read the
  argument" are wght 500. Masthead name 500.
- Deck: 1.0625rem/1.5, wght 400, grey; the hero caption is 1.125rem with the
  CTA ("Read the argument →") at 1.25rem, wght 500, black — the one action on
  the screen is not its smallest text.
- Article body: Geist 1.125rem/1.6, wght 400; lead-ins (`strong`) at 500,
  never bold. `code` inherits the body face at wght 500 (no mono anywhere).
- Article h2: 2.6rem, wght 400, wdth 80%, `opsz 60` — visibly medium; the
  hierarchy is size and condensation. "sources" / "comments" are small
  regular lowercase labels (the meta tier); lead-ins are plain; comment
  author names are regular with the grey date distinguishing them; the
  "next claim" title is one step lighter (600); the button is regular
  weight. Pull quotes track at −0.02em (the lead at −0.035em) so glyphs do
  not touch at that intermediate size.
- Focus ring: 3px solid black, 4px offset. Never the accent.

## Title size rule

**Lead (home).** Two layers. The CSS length tier below is the no-JS
fallback (verified to fit 1440×900: title bottom 555, caption bottom 860).
With JS, `fitLead()` measures a hidden clone placed exactly over the lead at
the final variable-axis values (the live title may still be settling) and
applies these rules:

- *Stable budget.* The fold is the 1440×900 reference, clamped 700–900px,
  so the title breaks identically on any taller viewport and shrinks only on
  short ones. Height-only resizes never refit; width changes do.
- *At most three lines.* A long title gets fewer lines at a smaller size,
  never a fourth stacked block.
- *Caption in the void.* Among sizes ≥ 75% of the largest that fits, prefer
  the one whose widest line comes closest to the rail *and* leaves a void of
  ≥ 20rem beside the last line (else beside the shortest line). The deck +
  CTA are then placed absolutely in that void, bottom-aligned to the line's
  baseline region, after a collision check against the other lines. If no
  such void exists, the caption sits under the title's left edge and the
  title gives up the caption's height (sizes ≥ 85% of cap, max fill).

Measured with the eight sample titles: five captions sit beside a line
(sample lead: title 234px, 3 lines, 99% fill, caption beside "The Future"),
three long titles fall back under the title.

**Wall.** One base size for every archive title (5.5vw ≈ 79px at 1440;
10vw at 390) — never a function of character count. Bounded exception,
applied by `fitWall()` on desktop only: a title that fits on ONE line may
grow up to 1.3× the base (binary search, still one line), so every band
carries comparable ink; two-line titles stay at base. Sample: one-liners at
98–103px, two-liners at 79px. On narrow screens only the title alternates
alignment; meta, deck, inline square and tags stay left for scanning.

**Post title.** Tier by character count (the article page has no fold job):

| tier | chars | lead fallback (home) | post title | lead ≤759px | post ≤759px |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| s | ≤ 30 | 12vw | 7vw | 18vw | 14vw |
| m | 31–45 | 12.5vw | 6vw | 18vw | 12vw |
| l | 46–60 | 10vw | 5vw | 15vw | 10vw |
| xl | > 60 | 8vw | 4.2vw | 12.5vw | 8.5vw |

`s` and `m` are nearly the same lead size on purpose: the measured glyph
advance at wdth 84 / wght 800 is ≈0.35em, so a ≤30-character title already
fills the width in one or two lines and anything larger wrapped it into
three short lines below the 70% fill floor.

**No single-word last line — a rule, not hand-setting.** The generator
binds the last two words of every title role (lead, wall, post, next) with a
no-break space (`glue()` in `c3.py`; trivially portable to the Astro
template). Chrome's `text-wrap: pretty` alone did not prevent "…is The /
Future" on the lead. The lead keeps `pretty` (ragged), wall and post titles
use `balance`; `overflow-wrap: break-word` is the safety net for an
unbreakable word.

Measured with the eight sample titles as the fitted lead at 1440×900: fill
90–100% of the content width, 2–3 lines, title 360–787px tall, caption
always inside the fold. At 390 (tiers, no fit): 2–4 lines, fill 74–97%, no
overflow.

## Accent derivation and measured contrast

Measured in headless Chrome by reading the computed colour of the real
elements (canvas-resolved to sRGB), then WCAG 2.x contrast:

| accent | highlight tint (`--accent-tint`) | black on tint | underline (`--accent-floor`) | underline on white |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#b4361f` (sample) | `rgb(255,213,198)` | 15.59:1 | `rgb(180,54,31)` | 6.02:1 |
| `#ffd400` (pale) | `rgb(249,232,171)` | 17.16:1 | `rgb(149,107,0)` | 4.79:1 |
| `#0a0a2a` (near-black) | `rgb(221,229,255)` | 16.73:1 | `rgb(16,17,50)` | 18.29:1 |

Tags and article links now underline in ink (`rgb(0,0,0)`, 21:1), so the
underline column documents the floored derivation only; the accent appears
exactly once per screen. The tint pins L at 0.93 and
caps chroma at 0.08, so black text stays ≥ 7:1 whatever the hue; the floor
clamps L to [0.2, 0.55] so a pale accent darkens to ≥ 3:1 and a near-black
one lifts slightly so it still reads as a colour.

One accent use per screen: home = the marker band in the deck (the wall has
no colour at all); article = the highlighted sentence in the opening
paragraph. Links stay visible at any hue because they no longer depend on
the accent.

## Composition notes

- Body reserves `padding-right: var(--rail)` on desktop so no content ever
  runs under the fixed nav or the vertical masthead. The masthead is
  absolute within the first screen (`min(100svh − 13rem, 49rem)` tall, text
  reads bottom-to-top from just above the nav); the nav cluster is fixed
  bottom-right (home · archive · rss · subscribe). Under 760px both collapse
  into a sticky white top strip: name (links home) + archive · rss ·
  subscribe.
- Hero: grid rows meta / title / 1fr / deck, `min-height: min(100svh, 62rem)`
  so the deck sits bottom-right of the first screen. On narrow screens there
  is no void: `min-height: auto`, the deck sits 2rem under the title and the
  wall follows.
- The wall: seven full-width bands, alternating `.band--left`/`.band--right`,
  separated by 6.25rem of whitespace only (rem, not vh, so the document
  height does not depend on the viewport: bands are ~260–320px tall, two fit
  a 900px viewport, home is 4,120px for eight stories). When the reveal has to sit below a full-width title it
  shrinks (240 → no less than 160px) so it never crosses into the next band.
- Article: a 41rem column (≈66ch of Geist) centred between the left edge and
  the rail (`--col-offset` = half the remaining width); pull quotes break
  out to the page edge and run wider than the column (up to 28ch at 4.4vw);
  the sources list runs in two columns inside the column, numbers on the
  column's left edge (a counter in a 2rem grid track, not list markers). Intro stack is two
  tiers: display standfirst (1.35rem) then body; the hook with its highlight
  is the first body paragraph.
- No `vh`-based spacing anywhere; the only viewport-relative heights are the
  hero (pinned in px by the fit script, `min(100svh, 62rem)` without JS) and
  the absolutely positioned masthead. Full-page capture tools grow the
  viewport to the document height, and anything else would inflate.

## Interaction

- Load: the lead title's axes settle once, 600ms, from wdth 100 / wght 300 to
  wdth 84 / wght 800 (CSS `@keyframes`, no JS). `prefers-reduced-motion:
  reduce` → no animation, final state only.
- Hover reveal (fine pointer, ≥760px): hovering a wall title shows its
  featured image as a fixed 240px square that follows the cursor
  horizontally with a light easing, hangs just below the title block (never
  over it), and shifts to the band's empty side if it would cover the
  deck/tags. Keyboard focus shows the same square pinned beside the title
  block, top-aligned and clear of its widest line (after it on left bands,
  before it on right bands) with one 24px gap, top-aligned to the widest
  line it sits beside, or on the band's empty side below the title if there
  is no room; it scrolls with the title, no easing. Reduced motion removes the
  easing and the fade. Touch / coarse pointers and narrow layouts never run
  the reveal; the inline 72px square is shown instead.
- Forms (comment, subscribe): `method="dialog"` so nothing is submitted with
  JS off; with JS, native validation runs, then a "Demo only — nothing was
  sent." status appears and the form resets.
- Article: nothing moves.

## Known gaps

- The featured-image-in-metadata path on the article page exists in the
  generator but is unexercised because the sample article has no image.
- The 90-degree masthead uses `writing-mode: vertical-rl` + `rotate(180deg)`
  rather than `sideways-lr` for broader browser support.
- RSS links to `#` because the static package has no feed file.
